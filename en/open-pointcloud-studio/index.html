View, measure and edit point clouds

Open Pointcloud Studio

From laser scan to dimension, drawing and mesh

A desktop application for viewing, measuring, editing and converting laser scans and other point clouds. From a scan it makes a section drawing in DXF or DWG, a closed mesh, and the planes and cylinders of a room. It runs on Windows, macOS and Linux, is written in Rust and needs no browser or web view.

Features

From scan to drawing

Start with the highlights or pick a topic.

01 Open and view

Large scans, on screen at once

LAS and LAZ open from their header, a large E57 file first shows a sample, and from one million points on the application builds an index on disk. It supplies the detail for the current viewpoint, up to ten million points in view, and is kept for next time.

Read more

02 Cut and measure

A section box that follows the walls

Six draggable faces limit what you see, select, export and draw. Type the limits, or turn the box with Align to walls along a building that stands at an angle in the scan. Measure distances and areas, also of a wall or a sloped roof.

Read more

03 Drawings

From scan to floor plan in DXF or DWG

A section drawing makes a plan or section at scale 1:1 of what the box cuts. The filled cut draws walls, columns and floors as regions and leaves door and window openings open; straight CAD lines add editable polylines. Open CAD Studio comes with it and shows the result at once, and on a sheet with a title block you place views at scale for a PDF.

Read more

04 Meshes

A closed mesh that tells you how good it is

Put the box around a room and make a surface without overlaps, closed wherever the scan has points and with door and window openings left open. Afterwards you see the mean, the 95% and the largest distance between the points and the mesh. Export as OBJ, PLY, STL, DXF, DWG or IFC.

Read more

05 Faces

Floors, walls and columns detected

Detect faces finds the flat faces of a room with their outlines, in which a window is a hole and a door a notch, and round columns and pipes as cylinders. For each face you see the type, the area and the residual of its points; export as JSON, OBJ, DXF, DWG or as IFC4 building elements.

Read more

  • Opening scans

    LAS, LAZ, E57, PLY, PCD, PTX, PTS and text, and meshes in OBJ, PLY, OFF and STL. Each file is a layer you show, hide or close.

  • A whole scan project at once

    A folder of scans, or the scans a scan project file (.rcp) lists, open together. Dropping them on the window works too.

  • Detail where you look

    An index on disk (an octree) supplies the detail for the camera position; the points stay on screen while you move.

  • Display

    Colour, elevation, intensity or class, eye-dome lighting, a view cube and perspective or orthographic.

  • Station photos and panoramas

    Stand in a scanner station, lay the photo over the points and give points the colours the photos see.

  • Photo beside the 3D model

    An E57 photo in a tab of its own, beside or above a 3D pane, with a draggable divider.

  • 3D BAG buildings

    Download building models of the Netherlands for an area in RD coordinates and show them with your scan.

  • Dutch and English

    The interface follows the language of the system and can be switched in Settings, with five colour themes.

  • Measuring

    Distance, horizontal length and height difference, and areas in their own plane, picked from all points of the scan.

  • Section box that turns

    Six draggable faces, typed limits and alignment to the walls; where it cuts a mesh, the cut looks solid.

  • Selecting and editing

    Select with a rectangle, delete with undo, thin, move and scale. The source file never changes.

  • Register scans

    Align scans with point pairs or a manual shift, refined with ICP, with residuals and a preview in deviation colours.

  • Exporting and merging

    The whole cloud, the selection or the section box as LAS, LAZ, E57, PLY, XYZ, PTS or CSV, and merging scans of mixed formats.

  • Views, notes and BCF

    A view keeps the camera, the section box and the notes; all views of a scan go out as one BCF 2.1 file.

  • Section drawing

    Plan, elevation or section in DXF or DWG, with a filled cut and straight CAD lines on a layer of their own.

  • Sheets to PDF

    A4 to A0 with a title block, views at scale, dimensions and text, exported as a PDF the size of the sheet.

  • Terrain and 3D surface

    Quick meshes of a scan, also as an OBJ with a texture (MTL and PNG) from the measured colours.

  • Closed mesh

    A surface without overlaps of a room, with the measured distance between the points and the mesh.

  • Detected faces

    Floors, ceilings, walls, sloped faces and round columns, as JSON, OBJ, DXF, DWG or IFC4.

  • Command API

    Every window listens on 127.0.0.1 and runs commands from scripts and other programs, only with a token.

  • Works with an AI assistant

    The MCP server gives an assistant a tool for every command and a screenshot of the 3D view.

  • Command line

    Convert, cut, merge, draw, mesh and detect faces without a window.

  • C library and cloud API

    The point-cloud core for software of your own, as a C library or as a service over HTTP and MCP in a container.

  • Extensions

    Extra tasks by other authors, as a program in any language that drives the window through the command API.

Frequently asked questions

What does it cost?

Nothing. Open Pointcloud Studio is open source: the desktop application is licensed under the GPL-3.0, the point-cloud core under the LGPL-3.0. No licence fees, no subscription and no account.

Which scans can I open?

LAS, LAZ, E57, PLY, PCD, PTX, PTS and text files with one point per line, and meshes in OBJ, PLY, OFF and STL. A folder of scans or a scan project file (.rcp) opens in one go; of such a project file only the list of scans is read, not the indexed scan copies (.rcs).

How large can a scan be?

For clouds of one million points or more, the application builds an index on disk. With it, scans of more than a hundred million points stay smooth: you see the detail of the current viewpoint, up to an adjustable maximum of ten million points in view. Such an index can take several gigabytes for a large survey.

Can I use the results in my CAD or BIM program?

Yes. Section drawings go to DXF or DWG, meshes and detected faces also as 3D geometry in DXF, DWG or IFC4, and saved views as BCF 2.1. Open CAD Studio comes in every package and shows an exported drawing at once. Opening DWG files is not possible.

Which systems does it run on?

Windows 10 and 11 (x64), macOS 11 and later on Apple and Intel processors, and Linux on x86-64 (Ubuntu 22.04, Debian 12, Fedora 36 or later). The packages for Linux on 64-bit ARM are experimental. There is no browser version.

Does it work with an AI assistant?

Yes. open-pointcloud-studio --mcp starts an MCP server with a tool for every command of the local command API: open scans, set the section box, measure, export, draw and mesh, and send back the 3D view as an image.

Can I use the core in my own software?

Yes. The point-cloud core is available as a C library for Windows, macOS and Linux and as a cloud API in a container image, both under the LGPL-3.0. From Rust you use the crate directly.

Which languages does the interface come in?

Dutch and English. The application follows the language of the system; in Settings you switch the language and pick one of the five colour themes.

How reliable are the section drawing, the closed mesh and face detection?

They were measured on generated rooms, not yet on a scan of a real building. A closed mesh and detected faces report how far the points lie from them, so you can judge the result yourself. The known limits are listed on this page under Technology.

Good to know
  • Lengths and areas are in metres. The coordinates of a scan are taken as metres and are not converted.
  • A measurement is not saved with the scan.
  • A 3D surface leaves holes and is not watertight.
  • A mesh holds at most four million vertices and eight million triangles, whether it comes from a file or from a mesh job.
  • Detected faces are not saved with the scan: export them to keep them. A scan holds one mesh; a new mesh replaces the previous one.
  • The point limit of a section drawing is 150,000 points by default and at most 400,000; when thinning to 5 mm leaves more points, the point spacing doubles until they fit.
  • The filled cut of a section drawing is traced from the points: a wall scanned from one side is drawn as a thin strip, gaps under about half a metre are closed, and furniture in the slab is drawn unless its points are deleted first.
  • A closed mesh rounds corners by about a quarter of a voxel, lets objects thinner than two or three voxels merge or get holes, and leaves some gaps beside another surface open. Without scanner stations, a surface that the centre of the region sees edge on can come out torn.
  • Detect faces reports no strips narrower than 0.15 m, such as door reveals. A region too large for the working budget is searched with larger voxels, thin scans give fewer faces or none, round columns wider than about 0.7 m come out as strips of wall, and without scanner stations the side a face looks at is a guess.
  • The filled cut, Closed mesh and Detect faces were measured on generated rooms, not yet on a scan of a real building. The user guide lists the limits of the filled cut, Closed mesh and Detect faces.
  • An index can take several gigabytes on disk for a large survey.
